## Designing Model planes

### Wing Cube Loading 
- This is relationship between weight of airplane , size of its wing and its speed. 
- Lighter airplanes with large wings become floaty , slow. Heavier airplanes with smaller wings are fast and slice through air. 
- The wing cube loading = $\frac{Weight(gram)}{{Wing \ Area(sq. \ decimeters)}^{1.5}}$  . A lower number makes it slow and floaty , whereas a higher number makes it fast and slice through the air.  2.5 is slow and floaty. The downside of a low WCL is that it is highly susceptible to winds. 16 is one which would slice through the air. 
- Adding weight and increasing WCL would make its flight time decrease , since the distance will be covered faster.

## Glider Flight Physics
- On a glider , we can consider a Lift vector perpendicular to trajectory , then a thrust vector along trajectory and anti lift perpendicular to trajectory opposite to lift vector. Thrust and anti lift are the components of the weight vector.  Drag acts in the opposite direction of the thrust. 
- To maximize the distance , we must maximize the glide ratio. Increasing weight of the glider does not change the distance. 
	- Lift to drag ratio L/D = Glider ratio. Therefore , we must minimize the drag force and increase the lift force. 


## Parasite Drag Force
- The pressure force acts perpendicular to the area. The drag force due to pressure on the object is called the pressure or form drag. 
- The turbulent wave at the back of the object is a area of low pressure , there is significantly lesser force pushing it forwards , causing a net backward force. 
- The resultant of pressure / form drag along with skin friction cause the parasite drag force on the object. 
- To minimize this drag force , we need to make a sort of tear drop shape. This process is called streamlining. This is done by tapering the edges. The air can flow around it pretty smoothly. The front must also be a bit pointed so as to make air flow a bit smoothly around it. This causes the air to change its direction gently and minimize the drag. 
- This resultant shape is called an airfoil.
- Airfoils will reduce the pressure drag and causes a good lift force. Skin friction is reduced by making the surface very smooth. 
- Drag = $$\frac{1}{2}\rho \ V^{2} \ S \ C_{d}$$ 
- $C_{d}$ is directly proportional to the Drag and is experimentally measured. It has no units. 

## Lift Force
- Lift = $$\frac{1}{2}\rho \ V^{2} \ S \ C_{l}$$
- In general $Lift \approx \ mg$ . $$\therefore  V = \sqrt{\frac{2*mg}{\rho \ S \ C_{l}}} $$
- Wing loading = $\frac{m}{S}$
- A cambered airfoil works better by deflecting the airflow downwards better than a symmetric one. Since the airflow is forced downwards more, by 3rd law , the equal and opposite force will be greater as well. It is generally shown by a percentage. A camber will increase the lift coefficient. 
- When the angle of attack is increased too much , there might be flow separation when the airflow might not be able to reconnect and generate meaningful lift , causing turbulence, where the drag becomes too much. It is generally between 10$\degree$ and 20$\degree$. 


## Induced drag Force
- There is high pressure on the bottom of the wing and low pressure on the top. This means that at the wing tips , the high pressure below will spill over the low pressure region like a small tornado. 
- Wing tip vortices will make the lift force weaker and produce additional drag force which is called the induced drag. 
- The downwards component of airflow is called downwash. This reduces lift and causes drag. It also decreases the angle of attack. 
- $$C_{d,i} = \frac{{C_1}^2}{\pi * AR * E}$$

- This means that the induced drag force decreases as we increase the speed and increases as we decrease the speed. On the other hand, parasite drag force will increase as the speed increases.

- Higher aspect ratio means that the downwash will be lesser towards the middle of the glider.

## Deep understanding of Lift
- The angle between the relative wind and chord line (line joining the leading and trailing edges) is called the angle of attack. 
- The camber defines how curved the airfoil is. 
- So when a fluid flows over the surface, we have 2 kinds of stresses, first we have the wall shear stress then the pressure stress. Lift is the resultant of these 2 stresses. 
- Integrating the stresses in the lift direction over the surface , gets us the lift force. 
- Pressure is low above the airfoil and high below it. Low pressure on the top surface is higher in magnitude than the high pressure on the bottom surface. 
- Airfoils are optimized to have a high lift to drag ratio. Bernoulli's principle , states that at the leading edge the v becomes zero. The fluid over the top surface is faster than at the bottom surface. This means that the increase in velocity will result in increased pressure over the top. The geometry of the airfoil will cause the wind to be pinched together towards the top but not below it, due to conservation of mass , it results in increase of velocity towards the top, thus. Circulation has the effect of the accelerating the flow above and decelerating the flow below it.(Kutta condition.)
- Newtons third law , the force put by the airfoil on the wind to create downwash must have a reaction force on it to create lift.  


## Reynold's Number
- The characteristic length is the chord length of the airfoil. $$Reynold's \ Number = \frac{L * V}{\nu}$$ Where , L is the chord length , V is the wind velocity and $\nu$ is the kinematic viscosity.
- Increasing Re. will reduce the parasitic drag. Therefore , we can reduce the parasitic drag by making them go faster or increasing the chord length. 

## Stepped Airfoils
- KFM airfoils are stepped and reduce skin friction, thus reducing the parasite drag. However this only works for smaller planes with lower Reynold's numbers.
